# Automotive Cybersecurity Briefing — 2026-11-15

**Window:** 2026-11-08 through 2026-11-15 (7-day default; America/New_York)

**Assessment:** Two fresh primary-source technical signals cleared the archive-controlled screen: ATHENE/Fraunhofer describes a centralized TPM 2.0 trust anchor for ISO 15118-20 Plug & Charge in zonal vehicle architectures, and a USPTO publication from Karma Automotive describes gateway/domain-controller message validation and split-key authentication for vehicle networks. Neither is evidence of a deployed vulnerability or exploit; both are architecture/watch items.

## Academic / research

- **Headline:** Centralized TPM 2.0 architecture for Plug & Charge in zonal vehicles
- **Publication date:** November 13, 2026 (publisher listing)
- **Source family:** Research institution / conference paper
- **Canonical source URL:** https://www.athene-center.de/forschung/publikationen/automotive-security-architectures-for-plug-charge-5499
- **Why it matters:** The proposal places key generation, storage, and use for multiple EV communication controllers inside one TPM 2.0 on a high-performance controller, using an EcoG/ISO 15118 framework with emulated EVCC and SECC devices. It directly targets private-key exposure and the scaling problem of repeated trust anchors in zonal architectures.
- **Decision note:** Treat centralized trust as an architecture trade-off, not a universal answer. For Plug & Charge designs, test TPM availability/failure handling, key-usage authorization between ECUs, compromise blast radius, recovery and provisioning, and whether the central controller becomes a single point of denial or trust concentration. Request the full paper before claiming demonstrated production performance.
- **Confidence:** Medium — publisher page and abstract are verified, but the page gives only year-level metadata and the design is a conference-paper proposal/emulation, not fleet deployment evidence.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; EV charging; Plug & Charge; academic research
- **Raw capture:** [ATHENE capture](../../../../sources/athene-automotive-security-plug-charge-tpm-2026-11-13.md)

## Patents / technical disclosures

- **Headline:** Karma Automotive vehicle-security publication uses cross-path validation and split key material
- **Publication date:** November 12, 2026
- **Source family:** USPTO primary patent publication
- **Canonical source URL:** https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/20260361706
- **Why it matters:** US 2026/0361706 A1 describes validating messages from a gateway/domain-controller architecture, including matching messages received over Ethernet and CAN within a time window, and splitting authentication-key material across messages/paths. The approach is aimed at making malicious modification of vehicle-network messages harder, including in CAN-FD and zonal-controller configurations.
- **Decision note:** Keep this as an implementation signal, not a validated control. Compare the claims with existing SecOC/MAC, gateway filtering, redundancy, and intrusion-detection designs; evaluate replay, coordinated compromise of both paths, timing manipulation, availability impact, key provisioning, and whether duplicated traffic creates new attack surface. A patent publication does not establish adoption, novelty against deployed systems, or measured security efficacy.
- **Confidence:** High for the publication date, assignee, and described claims; low-to-medium for practical effectiveness because no deployment or independent evaluation is shown.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; in-vehicle networks; CAN-FD; technical disclosure
- **Raw capture:** [USPTO capture](../../../../sources/uspto-karma-vehicle-security-system-2026-11-12.md)
