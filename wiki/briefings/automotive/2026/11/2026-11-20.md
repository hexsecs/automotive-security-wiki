# Automotive Cybersecurity Briefing — 2026-11-20

**Window:** 2026-11-13 through 2026-11-20 (7-day default; America/New_York)

**Assessment:** Three fresh primary-source signals cleared the archive-controlled promotion bar. ZDI added a sixth Grizzl-E Smart 40A advisory on November 19, covering unauthenticated OCPP UpdateFirmware handling and an out-of-bounds write that can support code execution. A new arXiv preprint proposes PhantoMasq, a graph-based detector for CAN masquerade attacks. A second arXiv paper documents a cybersecurity-dataset observatory and highlights thin public evidence for V2X and other vehicular-network domains. No new clearly automotive-specific CISA KEV promotion was identified.

## Vendor advisories / EV charging

- **Headline:** ZDI adds OCPP UpdateFirmware out-of-bounds-write advisory for United Chargers Grizzl-E Smart 40A
- **Publication date:** November 19, 2026
- **Source family:** Vulnerability researcher advisory — ZDI primary source
- **Canonical source URL:** https://www.zerodayinitiative.com/advisories/ZDI-26-1038/
- **Why it matters:** ZDI describes CVE-2026-131813 as a network-adjacent, unauthenticated flaw in OCPP UpdateFirmware handling that can write past an allocated buffer and support arbitrary code execution, in combination with other issues. It extends the prior Grizzl-E advisory cluster rather than standing alone.
- **Decision note:** Treat the six-advisory cluster as one asset-management workstream: identify deployed units, restrict OCPP/management exposure, confirm firmware and signing status, and obtain vendor remediation or compensating-control guidance. Do not infer exploitation in the wild or fleet-wide exposure from the advisory alone.
- **Confidence:** High for the advisory, product, CVE, date, and described condition; medium for practical exposure and remediation status.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; EV charging; advisories/CVEs
- **Raw capture:** [ZDI capture](../../../../sources/zdi-united-chargers-grizzle-smart-40a-updatefirmware-2026-11-19.md)

## Academic research / in-vehicle networks

- **Headline:** PhantoMasq targets CAN masquerade attacks with graph-based detection
- **Publication date:** November 19, 2026
- **Source family:** Academic preprint / arXiv primary source
- **Canonical source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26094
- **Why it matters:** PhantoMasq represents CAN traffic as message-sequence graphs plus signal-behavior features, then classifies five masquerade attack types. On the ROAD dataset, the authors report mean AUC-ROC from 0.831 to 0.999 under a split protocol designed to avoid overlapping train/test windows. This directly addresses the stealthy substitution problem highlighted by earlier CAN-security research.
- **Decision note:** Use it as a detector-design lead, not production evidence. Reproduce the split and test on vehicle-specific identifiers, timing drift, ECU changes, replay/coordination, and adversarial adaptation; measure latency, false positives, and containment impact alongside AUC.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high — canonical arXiv record, method, dataset, and reported range verified; independent and vehicle-specific validation remain open.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; CAN; intrusion detection; academic research
- **Raw capture:** [arXiv capture](../../../../sources/arxiv-2611-26094-phantomasq-can-masquerade-2026-11-19.md)

## Evidence quality / dataset governance

- **Headline:** Cybersecurity Dataset Observatory flags thin public evidence for V2X and vehicular-network AI security
- **Publication date:** November 16, 2026
- **Source family:** Academic preprint / arXiv primary source
- **Canonical source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20812
- **Why it matters:** The observatory catalogs 234 network-traffic datasets across 14 domains and reports limited public evidence for UAV, wireless, and V2X networks, while many datasets distribute only processed records. That matters because vehicle-security IDS and ML claims can look strong on benchmarks that do not represent deployed traffic, attacks, or sensor/network conditions.
- **Decision note:** Before adopting or comparing automotive IDS results, record dataset provenance, raw-versus-processed availability, vehicle/network realism, leakage controls, attack coverage, and independent test conditions. Treat benchmark numbers as deployment evidence only after reproducing them on representative data.
- **Confidence:** Medium — canonical arXiv abstract and reported catalog figures verified; catalog completeness and domain-specific conclusions should be checked against the observatory itself.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; V2X; AI security; research methodology
- **Raw capture:** [arXiv capture](../../../../sources/arxiv-2611-20812-cybersecurity-dataset-observatory-2026-11-16.md)
