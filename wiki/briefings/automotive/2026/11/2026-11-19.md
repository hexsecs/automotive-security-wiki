# Automotive Cybersecurity Briefing — 2026-11-19

**Window:** 2026-11-12 through 2026-11-19 (7-day default; America/New_York)

**Assessment:** The strongest fresh operational signal is a coordinated set of five ZDI advisories published November 18 for the United Chargers Grizzl-E Smart 40A EV charger. The disclosures describe network-adjacent, unauthenticated attack paths involving hard-coded OCPP-backend credentials, missing Wi-Fi configuration authentication, OCPP/FTP stack overflows, and firmware-download integrity failure. VicOne's November 19 analysis adds a forward-looking AI-defined-vehicle risk lens: manipulated inputs, model supply-chain issues, and agent permissions must be traced through to vehicle actions. No fresh automotive-specific CISA KEV promotion cleared the screen.

## Vendor advisories / EV charging

- **Headline:** Five ZDI advisories expose chained attack paths in United Chargers Grizzl-E Smart 40A
- **Publication date:** November 18, 2026
- **Source family:** Vulnerability researcher advisory / ZDI primary source
- **Canonical source URL:** https://www.zerodayinitiative.com/advisories/ZDI-26-1032/
- **Why it matters:** ZDI published five advisories for the same EV charger. The set includes hard-coded credentials in the OCPP backend configuration (CVE-2026-131811, CVSS 7.6), unauthenticated Wi-Fi configuration changes (CVE-2026-131808, CVSS 6.3), two stack-based buffer overflows (CVE-2026-131807 and CVE-2026-131812, each CVSS 7.5), and missing firmware integrity verification (CVE-2026-131810, CVSS 7.5). ZDI says the issues are network-adjacent, require no authentication, and can be chained toward arbitrary code execution; it also reports repeated unsuccessful attempts to establish a vendor disclosure channel.
- **Decision note:** Identify deployed Grizzl-E Smart 40A units and isolate management/OCPP paths from untrusted adjacent networks. Verify whether a vendor fix, firmware-signing remediation, credential rotation, or compensating control exists; ZDI's page lists restriction of interaction as the salient mitigation. Treat the chainability and exposure as product-specific until independently reproduced, and do not infer exploitation in the wild from a public advisory.
- **Confidence:** High for the five advisory records, CVEs, dates, affected product, and described conditions; medium for fleet-wide exposure and practical exploitability because deployment topology and vendor remediation status were not established here.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; EV charging; vendor advisories/CVEs
- **Raw capture:** [ZDI captures](../../../../sources/zdi-united-chargers-grizzle-smart-40a-advisories-2026-11-18.md)

## Researcher analysis / AI-defined vehicles

- **Headline:** VicOne maps AI-defined-vehicle risk from manipulated information to vehicle action
- **Publication date:** November 19, 2026
- **Source family:** Automotive researcher/vendor lab analysis — VicOne CyberThreat Research Lab
- **Canonical source URL:** https://vicone.com/blog/ai-defined-vehicles-securing-the-decisions-that-drive-vehicle-behavior/
- **Why it matters:** VicOne's analysis argues that AI-defined vehicles add attack paths through manipulated inputs, poisoned model supply chains, prompt injection, and excessive agent authority. It recommends extending TARA to models, agents, prompts, and action paths; tracking model provenance; enforcing authorization outside the model; monitoring model/agent behavior alongside CAN; and assigning ownership across safety, cybersecurity, and AI teams. The article cites prior research, including a ReasonBreak study, reporting up to 72% trajectory-manipulation success against Alpamayo 1 in closed-loop simulation.
- **Decision note:** Use this as a threat-modeling and governance input, not as evidence of a production vehicle compromise. For AI-enabled vehicle functions, add input-to-action abuse cases, model/data provenance, least-privilege enforcement, independent safety interlocks, and detection/containment tests to TARA and supplier reviews. Keep cited research results separate from VicOne's recommendations until the underlying papers are reviewed.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high — publisher date, recommendations, and cited examples were verified on the canonical page; the operational risk depends on each vehicle's architecture, model permissions, and safety controls.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; AI-defined vehicles; researcher analysis
- **Raw capture:** [VicOne capture](../../../../sources/vicone-ai-defined-vehicles-securing-decisions-2026-11-19.md)
