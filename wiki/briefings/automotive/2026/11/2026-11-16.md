## Academic research / privacy

- **Headline:** CANTraj demonstrates trajectory recovery from CAN signals despite GPS omission
- **Publication date:** November 13, 2026
- **Source family:** Academic preprint / arXiv (primary source)
- **Canonical source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18442
- **Why it matters:** CANTraj pairs decoded CAN events with time-aligned GNSS trajectories across 3,940 drives and 454 drivers. Under the paper’s access assumptions, adding steering to speed raised five-minute online route recovery from 56% to 87% when start and heading were known; offline recovery reached 88% with heading and 81% without it. The practical lesson is that removing GPS fields does not necessarily remove location privacy risk from shared vehicle telemetry.
- **Decision note:** Treat CAN-derived location as sensitive data in dataset release, telematics, usage-based insurance, and fleet-analytics reviews. Test whether steering, speed, event timing, road-network constraints, and known-origin metadata permit route reconstruction; minimize or coarsen high-risk signals and validate claims of “GPS-free” privacy against reconstruction attempts. Do not generalize the reported rates to every vehicle or dataset without reproducing the access assumptions.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high — canonical arXiv record, date, abstract, dataset scale, and headline results verified; external validation across OEM-specific CAN schemas and operational datasets remains open.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; vehicle-data privacy; CAN telemetry; academic research
