## Threat intelligence / researcher analysis

- **Headline:** Your Robot’s Safety Functions Already Work. What If the Input Lies?
- **Publication date:** November 11, 2026
- **Source family:** Automotive-security vendor research / VicOne LAB R7 (physical-AI and robotics security)
- **Canonical source URL:** https://vicone.com/blog/your-robots-safety-functions-already-work-what-if-the-input-lies-/
- **Why it matters:** VicOne argues that safety functions can behave exactly as designed while manipulated sensor, position, audio, visual, or other inputs drive the wrong decision. Its “safety trust anchor” framing is relevant to autonomous and AI-defined vehicles: redundant checks may provide less assurance when one manipulation can influence multiple inputs or when safeguards depend on the same untrusted signal.
- **Decision note:** Add adversarial-input cases to vehicle safety/security validation: trace every safety intervention to its input dependencies, test spoofed or coordinated inputs, verify an independent safeguard can detect the failure, and revisit evidence after model, sensor, software, or operating-condition changes. Do not treat the robotics demonstrations as proof of a vehicle exploit; use them as a transferable test-design prompt.
- **Confidence:** Medium — canonical publisher page and date verified; the source is vendor research and the demonstrated systems are robots, not production vehicles.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; AI-defined/autonomous vehicles; physical-AI safety
