## Academic research / vehicle control

- **Headline:** Model-free stealthy attack challenges vehicle IDS and ISO/SAE 21434 feasibility scoring
- **Publication date:** November 15, 2026
- **Source family:** Academic preprint / arXiv (primary source)
- **Canonical source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20293
- **Why it matters:** The paper reports a VARX-based false-data-injection attack learned from passive nominal measurements, without privileged dynamics, estimator, noise, or detector knowledge. In its closed-loop lane-keeping benchmark, the attack produced 1.25 m lateral displacement and 9.21 seconds outside the lane corridor; attack-period alarm rates were below 2.4% at a matched 1% false-alarm point across the evaluated monitors.
- **Decision note:** Revisit TARA assumptions that assign low feasibility because an attacker lacks detailed system knowledge. Validate the result against vehicle-specific sensor paths, estimator redundancy, actuator safeguards, and real-time IDS—not just the paper’s benchmark. Add detection-conditioned impact/feasibility analysis, and do not treat benchmark results as evidence of a production exploit.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high — canonical arXiv record, date, methods, and reported benchmark results verified; independent replication and vehicle-specific validation remain open.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; in-vehicle networks; safety-critical control; academic research

## Academic research / V2X privacy and model governance

- **Headline:** CULypso proposes clustered federated unlearning for vehicular ad-hoc networks
- **Publication date:** November 17, 2026
- **Source family:** Academic preprint / arXiv (primary source)
- **Canonical source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22682
- **Why it matters:** CULypso targets the practical problem of removing a client’s training influence from a mobile VANET model while preserving time and communication efficiency. The authors report approximately 65% improvement over FedEraser across time, server storage load, and unlearning fidelity in their experiments. This is relevant when vehicle or roadside data must be withdrawn under privacy or governance requirements.
- **Decision note:** Keep this as a research watch item, not a deployment recommendation. For vehicle ML systems, require deletion verification, poisoning/backdoor regression tests, cluster churn and disconnect tests, audit evidence, and checks that approximate unlearning does not retain sensitive or unsafe behavior. Compare against the actual model, federation topology, and legal retention requirements.
- **Confidence:** Medium — canonical arXiv record and abstract verified; the reported improvement is author-reported and not independently reproduced here.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; VANET; privacy; federated learning; academic research
