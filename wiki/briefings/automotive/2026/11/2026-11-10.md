# Automotive Cybersecurity Briefing — 2026-11-10

**Window:** 2026-11-03 through 2026-11-10 (7-day default; America/New_York)

**Assessment:** Two fresh, high-signal items cleared the archive-controlled promotion bar: primary researcher disclosure of a ForgeRock/OpenIDM deployment chain that reached connected-car commands, and an official CERT-In vulnerability note covering Tata Motors connected-car apps. The AAOS November bulletin and prior TARA/IDS/GNSS items remain watchlist context and are not counted as fresh. Direct arXiv screening did not surface a distinct automotive-security preprint that survived relevance and archive de-duplication.

## Threat intelligence / researcher analysis

- **Headline:** Hacking Two Automakers: ForgeRock Path Confusion, RCE, and Remote Vehicle Access
- **Publication date:** November 7, 2026
- **Source family:** Primary researcher disclosure
- **Canonical source URL:** https://samcurry.net/forgerock
- **Why it matters:** A deployment chain involving an exposed ForgeRock/OpenIDM administrative surface, default credentials, and WAF/path interpretation differences gave researchers production identity access for two automakers. In controlled testing, valid customer sessions reached connected-car APIs and enabled owner-equivalent telemetry and remote commands.
- **Decision note:** Audit every ForgeRock/PingIDM deployment for `/system/console`, default or staging credentials, edge/backend path discrepancies, exposed admin tooling, and secrets in mobile bundles. Validate customer-account-to-vehicle authorization as a separate trust boundary; do not assume WAF rules compensate for backend exposure. The automakers are unnamed and the U.S. chain was reportedly remediated quickly, so map findings to actual programs rather than infer broad fleet exposure.
- **Confidence:** High — canonical researcher write-up, timeline, affected technology, and controlled-test boundary verified; affected brands and current residual exposure are unknown.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; connected-car identity; threat intelligence

## Vendor advisories / CVEs

- **Headline:** CERT-In reports four High-severity flaws in Tata Motors iRA and iRA.ev apps
- **Publication date:** November 4, 2026
- **Source family:** Government vulnerability advisory / CERT-In
- **Canonical source URL:** https://www.cert-in.org.in/s2cMainServlet?VLCODE=CIVN-2026-0537&pageid=PUBVLNOTES01
- **Why it matters:** The official note covers older iRA 2.0 and iRA.ev builds and identifies hardcoded credentials/API material, unsafe WebView URL/origin handling, token exposure in logs, and ineffective certificate pinning. The apps provide access to connected-vehicle information and services; CERT-In recommends iRA 2.0 26.8.2 and iRA.ev 26.9.1.
- **Decision note:** Treat this as an application/backend identity boundary issue, not evidence of driving-system compromise or active exploitation. Confirm fleet/customer update coverage, remove debug logging and embedded secrets from release builds, retest WebView-to-native trust boundaries, and verify certificate-pinning behavior. Check whether the four CVEs map to any supplier SBOM or mobile release inventory.
- **Confidence:** High — official CERT-In note and affected/fixed versions verified; the advisory does not establish active exploitation or vehicle-control compromise.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; connected-car apps; advisories/CVEs
