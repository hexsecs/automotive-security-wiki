## Academic research / EV charging

- **Headline:** SAST coverage across languages is an OCPP implementation-security question
- **Publication date:** November 10, 2026
- **Source family:** Academic preprint / IEEE CSR 2026 paper (primary arXiv record)
- **Canonical source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14362
- **Why it matters:** The paper evaluates static-analysis tools across languages using OCPP implementations, a useful test of whether EV charging-station security assurance survives language and implementation differences. OCPP is specification-heavy and open to divergent interpretations; inconsistent SAST coverage can leave defects in charger software or charging-management components before deployment.
- **Decision note:** Treat this as a software-assurance input, not evidence that one SAST product is production-ready. For EVSE and CSMS programs, benchmark the actual languages, frameworks, generated code, protocol parsers, authentication/authorization paths, and CI configurations in use; combine SAST with dependency/SBOM review, protocol conformance tests, fuzzing, secrets scanning, and runtime validation. Request the full paper and reproduce findings on the organization’s OCPP codebase before changing tooling.
- **Confidence:** Medium — canonical arXiv record, date, venue, authors, and scope verified; the abstract-level capture does not support detailed comparative claims about tool efficacy.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; EV charging; academic research
