# Automotive Cybersecurity Briefing — 2026-11-04

**Window:** 2026-10-28 through 2026-11-04 (7-day default; America/New_York)

## Assessment

Two actionable automotive-security signals cleared the archive-controlled promotion bar. The November AAOS bulletin expands the previously noted passenger-display disclosure into a broader patch set: eight AAOS-specific CVEs are listed across Android 14–17, including six elevation-of-privilege and two information-disclosure issues. A new October 29 arXiv preprint evaluates privacy-preserving, post-quantum-authenticated federated reinforcement learning for vehicular networks. No automotive-specific CISA KEV addition appeared in the checked window; generic KEV entries remain deployment-context context rather than vehicle-sector evidence.

## Vendor advisories / CVEs

- **Headline:** November 2026 Android Automotive OS bulletin lists eight AAOS-specific CVEs
- **Publication date:** November 2, 2026
- **Source family:** Vendor advisory / Android Automotive OS bulletin
- **Canonical source URL:** https://source.android.com/docs/security/bulletin/aaos/2026/2026-11-01
- **Why it matters:** The official bulletin lists CVE-2026-49903, CVE-2026-49906, CVE-2026-58915, CVE-2026-58925, CVE-2026-58927, and CVE-2026-58930 as high-severity elevation-of-privilege issues, plus CVE-2026-58924 and CVE-2026-58926 as high-severity information-disclosure issues. The listed affected AOSP versions span Android 14, 15, 16, 16 QPR2, and 17, with device-specific firmware supplied by the OEM/device supplier. NVD’s date-bounded automotive keyword query independently returned CVE-2026-58915 and describes it as an unauthenticated passenger-display notification/action authorization failure.
- **Decision note:** Treat the bulletin as a patch-coordination item, not merely a single-CVE ticket. Obtain supplier firmware applicability for each AAOS branch, verify the 2026-11-01-or-later patch level, and test passenger/driver isolation, notification redaction, and PendingIntent authorization across multi-user/multi-display states. Do not infer exploitability or vehicle exposure from the bulletin alone.
- **Confidence:** High — official AAOS bulletin and NVD record were checked directly; affected vehicle programs and remediation timelines remain supplier-specific.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; Android Automotive OS; advisories/CVEs

## Academic research / arXiv

- **Headline:** Communication-Efficient Private Federated Reinforcement Learning with Post-Quantum Authentication for Vehicular Networks
- **Publication date:** October 29, 2026
- **Source family:** Academic preprint / arXiv (primary source)
- **Canonical source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41055
- **Why it matters:** The paper couples local differential privacy, one-bit model updates, and post-quantum-authenticated vehicle-to-infrastructure communication for cooperative vehicular routing. Under its simulated conditions, one-bit uploads reduce authenticated uplink size by 92.4%; correlated private quantization improves aggregation error and reported travel-time outcomes relative to the compared independent-quantization setup. This is relevant to privacy-preserving V2X/IoV learning where bandwidth, intermittent links, and authentication overhead compete.
- **Decision note:** Use it as a research input for privacy and crypto-agility experiments, not as production evidence. Ask for reproducible code/data, sensitivity to vehicle churn and adversarial clients, key/certificate lifecycle assumptions, and validation on real V2X stacks before relying on the reported gains.
- **Confidence:** Medium — canonical abstract/date and code link are verified, but the paper is a preprint and its claims are simulation-based.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; V2X/IoV; privacy; post-quantum authentication

## Vendor advisories / CVE validation

- **Headline:** No automotive-specific CISA KEV addition promoted
- **Publication/date window:** October 28–November 4, 2026
- **Source family:** CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog and NVD primary API validation
- **Canonical source URL:** https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/feeds/known_exploited_vulnerabilities.json
- **Why it matters:** The checked CISA additions were WordPress Core, Ivanti Connect Secure, GitLab, Cisco Secure Firewall, VMware ESX, and Linux Kernel. None is identified in the catalog as a vehicle, EVSE, telematics, infotainment, OTA, or automotive-platform product. The NVD `automotive` keyword query returned one result, CVE-2026-58915, already covered above.
- **Decision note:** Keep the negative result query-bounded. Map generic Linux, VMware, network, and CI/CD exposure against OEM supplier, fleet backend, charging-management, manufacturing, and cloud assets rather than promoting them as vehicle vulnerabilities without deployment evidence.
- **Confidence:** High for the retrieved feed/API slices; medium for sector coverage because product naming and deployment context can be implicit.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; advisories; CVE triage

## Bottom line

**Patch coordination plus research signal:** the AAOS bulletin is the operational priority—treat its eight-CVE set as a supplier patch and multi-display isolation workstream. The new vehicular federated-learning preprint is a useful privacy/crypto-agility lead, but remains simulation-only. No new automotive-specific KEV promotion or active-exploitation signal cleared the checked window.
