# Automotive Cybersecurity Briefing — 2026-11-14

**Window:** 2026-11-08 through 2026-11-14 (7-day default; America/New_York)

**Assessment:** Two fresh primary-source signals cleared the archive-controlled promotion bar: China’s automotive standards committee opened comment on an EV BMS technical-specification draft with an authorization/integrity angle for memory data, and LG Uplus announced ISO/SAE 21434 CSMS ML3 certification for its vehicle-software process. No new clearly automotive-specific CISA KEV promotion was identified. The ForgeRock connected-car disclosure, CATARC automotive-AI consultation, and SK hynix supplier-process item remain watchlist context and are not repeated as fresh.

## Regulation / standards

- **Headline:** China opens comment on EV BMS technical-specification draft with memory-data protection testing
- **Publication date:** November 10, 2026
- **Source family:** Government/standards primary source — CATARC / National Automotive Standardization Technical Committee
- **Canonical source URL:** https://zxd.catarc.org.cn/zxd/seekPublicAdvice/pagePublishAdviceStdList/964
- **Why it matters:** The official notice opens consultation on a recommended national-standard draft for EV battery-management systems. The linked draft and explanatory material add a test boundary for legitimate versus unauthorized commands to read or modify BMS memory data, tying cybersecurity protection to a safety-relevant control unit.
- **Decision note:** Track this as a standards watch item, not a final requirement. For BMS programs, map memory read/write authorization, diagnostic/service paths, secure boot/update controls, and audit evidence to the draft once the Chinese text is fully reviewed. Do not treat the consultation as mandatory compliance or infer that all deployed BMS products already meet it.
- **Confidence:** High for the consultation notice and linked official documents; medium for the security interpretation because the detailed draft/explanatory text is Chinese-language and the proposal is not final.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; EV/BMS; regulation/standards

## Vendor / supply-chain security

- **Headline:** LG Uplus announces ISO/SAE 21434 CSMS ML3 certification
- **Publication date:** November 10, 2026
- **Source family:** Vendor primary announcement — LG Uplus / LG newsroom
- **Canonical source URL:** https://lg.co.kr/media/release/30764
- **Why it matters:** LG Uplus says TÜV Rheinland assessed an ISO/SAE 21434-based cybersecurity management system at ML3, including application to actual vehicle-infotainment software projects. This is a useful supplier-assurance signal as OEMs demand evidence that security processes are practiced in delivery, not merely documented.
- **Decision note:** Treat the announcement as process evidence to validate, not as proof of product security. For supplier due diligence, request certificate scope, assessed sites and projects, surveillance status, vulnerability-management obligations, and integration evidence for the specific IVI/telematics products in use. Keep certification claims separate from deployment-level test results.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high — direct company announcement and date verified; certificate scope and customer-specific effectiveness remain company-reported and were not independently audited here.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; supplier security; ISO/SAE 21434; SDV

Track both as inputs to requirements and supplier reviews—not as proof of deployed protection.
