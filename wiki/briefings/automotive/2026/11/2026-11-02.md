## Academic research / arXiv

- **Headline:** Integrating of Attack Patterns and Threat Modeling to Support Security Analysis in the Automotive Domain
- **Publication date:** October 29, 2026
- **Source family:** Academic preprint / arXiv (primary source)
- **Canonical source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40944
- **Why it matters:** The paper proposes grounding automotive TARA in ISO/SAE 21434 while using STRIDE and CAPEC to make attack-scenario identification more systematic. Its Bluetooth-based automotive headlamp case gives a concrete example of connecting threat categories, attack patterns, and risk assessment.
- **Decision note:** Consider STRIDE/CAPEC mapping as a repeatable input to TARA and supplier reviews, especially for Bluetooth and other externally reachable component interfaces. Treat the contribution as a conceptual model plus case study—not as demonstrated production exploitability or validation of every CAPEC mapping.
- **Confidence:** Medium — canonical abstract and submission date verified; the paper is a preprint and the abstract supports methodology, not operational effectiveness.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; TARA; threat modeling

- **Headline:** Beyond Smart Homes: Characterizing IoT Vulnerabilities Across Sectors, Vendors, and Products
- **Publication date:** October 30, 2026
- **Source family:** Academic empirical study / arXiv (primary source)
- **Canonical source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42032
- **Why it matters:** Across more than 15,000 IoT CVEs, the study reports that weakness profiles vary by sector and that the automotive slice is characterized by memory issues. It also finds stronger similarity across products from the same vendor than across the same product type from different vendors, making vendor history and shared-code practices relevant to remediation prioritization.
- **Decision note:** Add vendor-level disclosure history, shared-code lineage, and memory-safety evidence to automotive supplier risk triage. Do not turn the sector-level classification into a vehicle-specific vulnerability claim; validate the paper’s automotive labeling and map the result against actual component and supplier inventories.
- **Confidence:** Medium — direct arXiv source and publication date verified; sector labels rely on a research classification pipeline and the result is not an exploit disclosure.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; vulnerability research; supplier risk
