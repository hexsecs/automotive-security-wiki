# Automotive Cybersecurity Briefing — 2026-11-27

**Window:** November 21–27, 2026, through 8:39 a.m. Detroit time.

**Assessment:** A new vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) paper examines physical-layer confidentiality when channel estimates go stale. The advisory and standards searches yielded no verified new automotive item beyond coverage already in the archive.

## Academic research / V2I confidentiality

- **Headline:** Predicting channel conditions for roadside links as vehicles move.
- **Publication date and source family:** November 27, 2026. Scientific Reports, early publication of an accepted journal article.
- **Canonical source:** [Publisher article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-80785-7), DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-80785-7.
- **What changed:** Khan and colleagues jointly optimize roadside beamforming and a surface that can transmit and reflect radio signals. Their numerical evaluation reports that the predictive design better preserves aggregate secrecy rate as vehicle speed and the interval between channel updates increase.
- **Why it matters:** A confidentiality analysis for this kind of [[concepts/v2x-security|V2X deployment]] should account for how quickly a moving vehicle makes a roadside transmitter's channel estimate obsolete.
- **Decision note:** Read if evaluating directional V2I radios or programmable surfaces. Check whether feedback delay, mobility and the passive listening model match the target environment. Keep protocol authentication and encryption in the design.
- **Confidence:** High for the publication and abstract-level result. The reported result is numerical. Direct full-text retrieval was unavailable, so the evaluation details and field applicability remain unverified.
