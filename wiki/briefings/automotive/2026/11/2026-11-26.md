# Automotive Cybersecurity Briefing — 2026-11-26

**Window:** November 20–26, 2026, through 8:31 a.m. Detroit time. The UNECE revision was released during the window and reports status as of November 19.

**Assessment:** A new V2X paper examines what traffic estimators can recover when position measurements are untrustworthy. A revised UNECE status table also provides a provisional date to track for the adopted UN R156 supplement.

## Academic research / V2X data integrity

- **Headline:** Traffic-state estimation under falsified V2X positions.
- **Publication date and source family:** November 25, 2026, online. Journal article in Computers, Materials & Continua.
- **Canonical source:** [Publisher article](https://www.techscience.com/cmc/online/detail/29211), DOI 10.32604/cmc.2026.092305.
- **What changed:** Li, Wang, and Ji describe a physics-informed graph state-space method for recovering coarse traffic observations. The publisher abstract reports recovery under large position-only displacements in the evaluated datasets.
- **Why it matters:** Aggregate traffic estimation can be assessed separately from the trustworthiness of an individual position observation. This distinction matters when choosing fallback behavior in [[concepts/v2x-security|V2X systems]].
- **Decision note:** Read for infrastructure-side estimation under untrusted inputs. Verify whether the underlying evidence covers the intended traffic conditions and the estimator's acceptable error before using the result in engineering decisions.
- **Confidence:** High for the publication date and abstract-level claim. Direct full-page retrieval was denied, so the evaluation methodology, baselines, and transfer to deployed systems were not verified.

## Regulation / UN R156 supplement

- **Date and source family:** UNECE WP.29 revised status table, snapshot as of November 19, 2026.
- **Canonical sources:** [Revised status table](https://unece.org/sites/default/files/2026-11/WP.29-200-30r1e.pdf) and [earlier status table](https://unece.org/sites/default/files/2026-11/WP.29-200-32e.pdf).
- **What changed:** The revised table lists December 27, 2026 as the possible entry-into-force date for Supplement 1 to the original version of UN R156, document 2026/169. The earlier November 17 snapshot lists January 28, 2027. The revised row leaves the definitive notification column blank.
- **Why it matters:** The revised date is a milestone to track for software-update management and type-approval planning.
- **Decision note:** Treat December 27 as provisional. Confirm the definitive depositary notification and jurisdictional applicability before updating compliance commitments.
- **Confidence:** High for the dates and status shown in the tables. Final entry into force was not independently verified.
