# Automotive Cybersecurity Briefing — 2026-10-18

## Question
What high-signal automotive cybersecurity developments appeared in the October 11–18, 2026 window?

## Short answer
The strongest current signal is a movement toward direct vehicle-domain exposure: VicOne’s observed Q3 dataset puts in-vehicle systems ahead of enterprise IT, while a new AV-security survey highlights thermal sensing and compute-management interfaces as under-modeled attack surfaces. Neither source establishes widespread exploitation.

## Evidence
- software-defined-vehicles — VicOne’s data supports treating vehicle, backend, and supply-chain monitoring as one operational risk surface. ^[sources/vicone-q3-2026-automotive-cyber-incidents.md]
- automotive-can — The Q3 findings reinforce the need to look beyond CAN-only controls toward in-vehicle endpoint and access-control monitoring. ^[sources/vicone-q3-2026-automotive-cyber-incidents.md]
- securing-autonomous-vehicle-systems-twin-aware-federated-reinforcement-learning — The thermal-attack survey extends AV threat modeling beyond network and model-poisoning concerns into sensor and silicon interfaces. ^[sources/thermal-side-channel-perception-attacks-autonomous-vehicles-2026-10-15.md]

## Decision note
Prioritize fleet telemetry/VSOC visibility and include thermal-IR spoofing plus thermal/DVFS telemetry leakage in AV security and SOTIF review checklists. Keep confidence at medium because VicOne’s observations are proprietary and the arXiv paper identifies limited on-target validation.
