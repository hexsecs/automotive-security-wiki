**Window:** 2026-10-23 through 2026-10-30 (7-day default; America/New_York)

**Assessment:** Two fresh academic signals cleared the archive de-duplication bar: a comparative post-quantum-cryptography evaluation for automotive V2V/V2X communications, and the previously identified Janus Vehicles threat model for protocol-conformant vehicles under a malicious-manufacturer assumption. No clearly automotive-specific CISA KEV/NVD item was promoted; generic ICS and enterprise advisories remain context rather than vehicle-sector evidence.

## Academic research / arXiv

- **Headline:** The use of post-quantum cryptography in the automotive context
- **Publication date:** October 28, 2026
- **Source family:** Academic preprint / arXiv (primary source)
- **Canonical source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38632
- **Why it matters:** The paper compares ML-KEM/Kyber, ML-DSA/Dilithium, and FALCON in simulated rural V2V and urban V2X-with-roadside-unit scenarios, against a conventional ECDSA/ECDH baseline. It reports low KEM latency for Kyber, balanced signature performance for Dilithium, and higher key-generation/signing costs but efficient verification for FALCON.
- **Decision note:** Treat this as an architecture-screening input for long-lived vehicle and roadside cryptography, not as production validation. Ask suppliers to expose message-size, latency, CPU/RAM, key-rotation, and downgrade/migration assumptions before using the results in a crypto-agility or V2X roadmap.
- **Confidence:** Medium — canonical abstract and submission date are verified, but the evidence is simulation-based and the paper is a preprint.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; V2X; post-quantum cryptography

- **Headline:** Janus Vehicles: Entity-Centric Threat Modelling of Protocol-Conformant Vehicles under a Malicious-Manufacturer Assumption
- **Publication date:** October 27, 2026
- **Source family:** Academic preprint / arXiv (primary source)
- **Canonical source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37059
- **Why it matters:** The model covers vehicles and components that remain authentic and protocol-conformant while performing undeclared sensing, communication, or context-triggered actuation. It identifies a gap between authenticity and declared-purpose conformance, including reappropriated genuine components that retain valid identities outside authorized contexts.
- **Decision note:** Add declared-purpose conformance, provenance, and context-limited capability checks to TARA and supplier-assurance reviews. This is a conceptual threat-model contribution, not evidence of a demonstrated production attack or prevalence.
- **Confidence:** Medium — primary abstract and date verified; operational exploitability is not established.
- **Lens:** Automotive cybersecurity; supply chain; V2X/entity assurance
